One hour in your own gym gets you enough footage for a year. The mistake is filming without a list and coming away with six clips of the same thing.

## The b-roll checklist

Five to eight seconds each, no talking, phone horizontal **and** vertical.

**Reception**
```
Someone being greeted at the desk
The academy poster on the wall
A member scanning the QR code
Hands taking a handout off the desk
```

**Gym floor**
```
A coach correcting someone's form
Two people talking mid-set, laughing
A member spotting a friend
Wide shot of the floor, busy
Weights being racked
```

**Coaching**
```
Coach demonstrating a movement
Coach watching, arms folded, concentrating
The moment after a good set
A high five, a fist bump — the real reaction, not a posed one
```

**Studying**
```
Laptop open on a café table
Notes and a coffee
Phone in hand, learning between sets
Someone studying in the gym after training
```

**People**
```
Portrait of each coach, 5 seconds, looking at camera
Each coach walking through the gym
Someone in gym uniform who clearly works there
```

## Getting the talking clips

Set up in the quietest corner. Coach sits or stands, phone on a shelf at chest height, window light on their face.

Ask the ten questions from *50 reel ideas*, one at a time. **Don't script the answers.** Ask, let them talk, move on. If they fumble the first one, keep it — that one is usually the most watchable.

## Three things people get wrong

**Filming vertical only.** You'll want horizontal for the gym TV screen. Do both while you're set up; it costs nothing.

**Wiping the b-roll.** The talking clips get used once. The b-roll gets reused for two years behind every caption you ever write. Keep it somewhere you'll find it.

**Waiting for a quiet gym.** A busy gym looks like a gym people want to be in. Film at 6pm, not 2pm.
