The graphics and the words are already done for you in **Meta ads — your ad pack**.
This is everything else: what to click, who to show it to, what to spend, and how to
tell whether it worked.

Budget is yours and so is the ad account. Nobody is asking you to spend anything — the
free routes in your playbook work. This is for when you want to put money behind it.

## Before you start

You need a Meta Business account, an ad account with a payment method on it, and
access to the {{gymName}} Facebook and Instagram pages. If you already boost posts,
you have all three.

## 1. Objective: Traffic

Not Leads. Not Sales.

Your ad sends people to your academy page, and the tracking on that page belongs to
us, not to your ad account. So your Ads Manager physically cannot see who enrolled,
and if you choose Sales it will optimise towards a signal it never receives. You will
pay more and get less.

Choose **Traffic**, optimise for **landing page views** rather than link clicks, and
judge it on enrolments in your portal rather than on anything Meta shows you.

*If you would rather your own account could see conversions properly, ask us — we can
share the tracking with your ad account. It is a one-off setup per gym and worth doing
if you plan to spend every month.*

## 2. Targeting

| Setting | Use |
|---|---|
| Location | Drop a pin on {{gymName}}, radius **4 miles** |
| Age | **18 – 40** |
| Gender | All |
| Detailed targeting | **Leave it empty to begin with** |
| Placements | Advantage+ (automatic) |

Four miles around one gym is already a small audience — in most towns somewhere
between ten and twenty thousand people once you have applied the age range. Stacking
fitness interests on top of that can cut it to a few thousand, which burns out in days
and costs more per person.

Start with location and age only. Add interests later **only** if the traffic you get
is obviously the wrong sort.

⚠️ Do not use the "job" or "employment" wording anywhere in the ad. Meta reads that as
a job advert, and a job advert loses interest targeting, loses lookalikes and is forced
to a **15km minimum radius** — which is four times the area you actually want. The copy
in your ad pack is already written to stay clear of this. See the rule at the bottom of
that entry.

## 3. Budget

There is no right number, and anyone who gives you one is guessing. What is not a guess
is your break-even:

**Every learner who enrols through your academy is £500 to you.** So the ad pays for
itself at one learner per £500 spent. At £10 a day that is fifty days. At £15 a day it
is a month.

A sensible first test is **£10 a day for two weeks**, one ad set, both creatives in it.
That is £140 to find out whether anyone in your town responds. If nobody clicks, you
have learned something cheaply. If people click and nobody enrols, the problem is the
page or the price, not the ad.

Do not start and stop it. Meta needs a few days of steady delivery before it settles,
and switching off on day three teaches you nothing.

## 5. Judging it

Look at three things, in this order:

1. **Enrolments in your portal.** The only number that pays you.
2. **Conversations.** Someone mentioning the ad at reception counts, and it will not
   appear in any report.
3. **Cost per landing page view** in Ads Manager. Useful for comparing your two
   creatives against each other. Useless as a measure of whether the campaign worked.

Give it two weeks before you judge it. A local audience this size takes a few days to
deliver evenly.

## Two things that will quietly break it

**Boosting instead of building.** The boost button on a post cannot do radius
targeting, cannot run two creatives, and optimises for engagement — likes from people
who will never enrol. It costs the same. Build it properly in Ads Manager.

**Letting it run untouched for months.** A four-mile audience sees the same image over
and over. Swap the creative when your monthly pack lands and it stays fresh for free.
