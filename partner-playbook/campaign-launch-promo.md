If you've been given a launch offer — **£500 off**, then **£300 off** — it exists for one reason: to get your first enrolment while the academy is still new enough that people are asking about it.

It is not a discount strategy. It's a deadline, and the deadline is what does the work.

## Run it in two waves

**Weeks 1–2 — £500 off**
The biggest number you'll ever put in front of your members. Lead with it, and say plainly that it's for the first intake only.

```
We've just opened our own Personal Training academy — and for the first two
weeks only, it's £500 off.

If becoming a PT has ever crossed your mind, this is the cheapest it will
ever be.

Ask at reception or scan the QR code.
```

**Weeks 3–4 — £300 off**
The drop is the message. People who ignored £500 off now know the offer is genuinely going, and that's what converts the ones who were waiting.

```
Last chance on the launch offer.

It drops to £300 off this week, then it's gone.

If you've been meaning to ask about the academy, ask this week.
```

## Where it goes

Both promo graphics are in your Resources. Put them:

- On the gym screen, in rotation
- On Instagram feed and Stories — the Stories version does more, because the countdown sticker works there
- On your poster, at the front desk
- In one email to the whole member list, week one

## Four rules

**Give a real end date.** "Limited time" converts nobody. "Ends Sunday 14th" converts. And if you say Sunday, it ends Sunday — an offer you quietly extend teaches your members to ignore every future one.

**Don't lead with the discount in conversation.** On a poster the number does the work. Face to face, ask the question first — *"have you ever thought about doing this professionally?"* — and mention the offer once they're interested. Nobody changes career because it's £500 cheaper; the discount only moves people who were already curious.

**Never invent a number.** Only quote the offer you were actually given, and only while it's live. If someone asks about a promo that's finished, say it's finished — don't improvise a replacement.

**Tell your team the dates.** The most common failure is a member asking about the offer at the desk on the last day and being told nobody knows anything about it.

## What good looks like

One enrolment from a launch promo is a good outcome — that's £500 to the gym and a member whose career changed. Two is a strong one.

Judge it on **conversations and enquiries**, not just enrolments. Somebody who asks during the launch and enrols in March still came from this campaign.
